Fabra

Making 3D fashion design simple, accessible and browser-based

FABRA

Fabra is a browser-based fashion design platform that lets designers create, visualize and prepare garments for production without desktop CAD software.

Solo-designer | 2025–2026

01 — The challenge

Everyone wants to start a fashion brand. But do they know what it takes to actually make one?

Getting a fashion idea out of your head and into production is a lot more complicated than it looks.

There’s the design itself, visualising how it’ll actually look, figuring out the technical details and then communicating all of that clearly enough for someone to make it.

And even experienced fashion teams deal with this complexity. The tools are powerful, but they can be hard to learn, workflows are spread across different software and getting from design to production often involves a lot of back and forth.

That’s where Fabra saw an opportunity. What if creating a production-ready fashion product could feel as intuitive as designing in Canva?

Tools like CLO3D and Style3D can do a lot, but they also come with a steep learning curve. Fabra wanted to keep the power designers need, while making the experience much easier to pick up and use, all from the browser.

That became the challenge: how could I bring Canva-like simplicity to professional 3D fashion design, without taking away the depth designers need to get their ideas into production?

02 — The approach

Fashion designers already know how to design. So why should they have to learn how software thinks?

Since Fabra was still in beta, we didn’t have a huge pool of users to learn from. Instead, I got close to the 30+ fashion designers in our private beta community to understand how they worked and where Fabra could make things easier.

I ran an in-person workshop with 10 designers, using hands-on activities to uncover their biggest pain points, frustrations and opportunities.

Someone opening Fabra for the first time shouldn’t need a tutorial just to get started. We wanted it to feel simple from the beginning, with more advanced tools there when designers actually needed them.

Being in the browser meant designers could just open Fabra and start working. No complicated setup, expensive hardware or being tied to one machine.

We also saw an opportunity to make the product itself part of the learning process. Templates and presets gave designers a useful starting point, while helping them understand how a production-ready garment comes together.

03 — Key decisions

One garment, one source of truth, from sketch to spec.

The defining decision was structural. Instead of separate tools for designing, patterning and specifying, Fabra treats the garment as a single living object. The Design Editor, Pattern Studio and Tech Pack are different views of the same source of truth, so a change made anywhere is reflected everywhere.

That decision removed the most painful ritual in fashion production: manually keeping documents in sync.

Other calls followed. We kept the 3D viewport centre stage but made every advanced control opt-in, trading feature visibility for approachability.

We built the Asset Library and Repeat Print Maker as shared foundations rather than bolt-ons, so every part of the product draws from the same materials.

And we priced experimentation into the product itself, with a free tier and AI credits, so trying an idea never feels expensive.

04 — The solution

A complete studio in the browser, simple by default and powerful on demand.

Fabra V2 shipped as a full ecosystem: a Design Editor for creating and visualising garments in 3D, Pattern Studio for construction, Tech Pack for production-ready specification and Range Plan for building collections, with an Asset Library, Repeat Print Maker and AI tools woven through the workflow.

The experience holds the original promise. A first-time designer can go from blank canvas to a visualised garment in a single session, while professional teams keep the precision, documentation and handoff rigour they came from CLO3D and Style3D expecting.

Every advanced capability is one intentional step away, never in the way.

05 — The impact

Launched, live and holding its promise: fashion design that starts in the browser.

Fabra V2 launched publicly in mid 2026, moving from free early access to live paid plans across Free, Starter and Pro tiers within a week. The launch validated the core bet: designers onboard themselves, without training, documentation or sales calls.

As the solo designer across the ecosystem, I shipped the end-to-end experience: product UX, marketing pages, onboarding, billing flows and the verbal identity they share.

The measure I care most about is behavioural. Designers stopped asking how to use Fabra and started asking what to make with it.
