Overview Context Discovery Direction Testing Perspectives Experience Outcome Learnings

Redesigning Freelancer.com profiles to help freelancers attract more projects and hires.

Freelancer.com is a global marketplace trusted by more than 71 million users.

Product Designer | Jan 2023 - Jul 2023

01 · Context

The profile had years of decisions behind it.

Freelancer's profile had evolved through years of product changes, new features and A/B testing. Much of what was there existed for a reason, even if the experience no longer felt particularly cohesive.

I was working within Freelancer's existing Bits design system, an established page structure and early concepts from my product manager.

The profile had also been revisited a couple of times before, but neither direction had made it into development. It meant there was already plenty to learn from as I started working through what should stay, what could change and where the experience could be improved.

02 · Problem discovery

The profile already had plenty of information. The hierarchy was the problem.

A freelancer's profile contains most of what a client might want to know: previous work, ratings, reviews, skills, badges and completed projects.

But those elements had accumulated over time and competed for attention. For someone seeing a freelancer for the first time, it was difficult to quickly understand what they were good at, what they had done before and whether they felt credible enough to hire.

For freelancers, their work also felt secondary to the information around it. The profile behaved more like a marketplace listing than somewhere they could properly present what they could do.

That shifted the problem for me. Rather than asking what else we could add to the profile, I started looking at how the information already there could work harder.

03 · The direction

Give the work more weight, without losing the trust freelancers had already earned.

Making the profile feel more like a portfolio seemed straightforward, but there was something important we couldn't lose.

Freelancers had spent years building ratings, reviews, completed projects and badges. Those signals gave clients information that the portfolio alone couldn't, so removing them for the sake of a cleaner profile would have solved the wrong problem.

The work should demonstrate capability, while reputation should reinforce trust.

04 · Testing the direction

Sharing the work early changed the design.

With two previous redesign attempts already stalled, I brought stakeholders in early. We used rough wireframes to work through the overall hierarchy, then moved into more detailed flows as we figured out how people would navigate and manage their profiles.

That process led to one of the bigger changes: how freelancers edited their profile.

Previously, making a change meant leaving the profile and going into a separate settings page. I brought editing into the profile itself, so freelancers could make changes where they would actually appear.

05 · Working through different points of view

Research and leadership weren't always pointing in the same direction.

As the designs developed, what we were hearing from users didn't always line up with what leadership had in mind for the profile.

Instead of choosing one direction over the other, I went back to what clients actually needed from the page: understand someone's work, decide whether they trusted them and know what to do next.

Those became a more useful way to judge the designs than which version people preferred.

The final direction took something from both. Leadership helped shape the overall character of the profile, while research informed how we prioritised portfolio work, profile management and trust signals.

06 · Designing the experience

Bringing the profile together.

Put the work first

Portfolio work became the focal point of the profile, with larger imagery and more space for freelancers to show what they could do.

Edit in context

Editing previously opened in a modal, pulling freelancers away from the profile itself. I brought those controls directly into the page, so they could make changes and see the result straight away.

Keep trust easy to scan

Ratings, completed projects and badges stayed visible alongside the work, giving clients supporting context without competing for the same attention.

Push the visual language

I pushed Freelancer’s existing visual language further with brighter colours and more expressive layouts, while keeping it familiar within the Bits design system.

07 · The outcome

A direction we could move forward with.

Research gave us confidence in the direction. Freelancers wanted their work to have more prominence and an easier way to manage their profile, while clients consistently looked for enough information to feel confident about who they were hiring.

Those findings shaped the final direction and became the foundation for the next phase of the profile redesign.

I moved on from Freelancer.com before the redesign launched, so I wasn't there to measure its post-launch impact.

08 · What I learned

Knowing what to keep matters as much as knowing what to change.

Working on a mature product changed how I think about redesigns. Something looking dated isn't always a reason to replace it. Existing patterns can carry years of user behaviour, experiments and expectations that aren't immediately obvious from the interface.

The harder part is knowing what still serves a purpose, and what's simply there because it's always been there.

Sometimes good design is less about starting again and more about giving what's already there a better chance to work.
