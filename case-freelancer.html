Freelancer.com

Optimising Freelancer.com's user profiles to attract more projects and hires.

Optimising Freelancer.com's user profiles to attract more projects and hires.

Product Designer | Jan 2023 - Jul 2023

01 · Context

Building on top of a legacy structure

The page's overall structure was legacy, already shaped by rounds of A/B testing before I joined, and I built inside Freelancer's existing Bits design system rather than starting from scratch.

My product manager had already sketched a few early ideas to work from.

02 · Discovery

Trust starts before a client even reaches out

The design dated back to the early 2000s, and clients noticed. A profile is often the first real signal someone gets about whether a freelancer's worth hiring, and an outdated one made that call harder than it needed to be, no matter how good the person behind it actually was.

Two earlier attempts to redesign the page had already stalled, not for lack of good ideas, but because it's a hard sell to get engineering time for a page that doesn't directly move revenue.

03 · Hypothesis

Bring people in early, and trust follows

If the profile could build trust before a client ever sent a message, and if stakeholders were brought into the design early enough to shape it themselves, this attempt could get further than the two before it.

This wasn't about making things look pretty. It was about giving clients a reason to trust someone before any conversation happened.

04 · Validation

Wireframes first, colour later

I turned early sketches into low-fidelity wireframes and shared them right away, so stakeholders were shaping the direction from day one instead of reacting to a finished design later. Mid-fidelity wireframes tested navigation and flow before any visual design work, surfacing two real changes, simplifying the manage-portfolio flow, and adding a clear call-to-action on the homepage.

Once the concept was validated, I used Midjourney to generate high-fidelity visuals instead of waiting on production-ready assets, which let me put a near-final version in front of people fast and get wider buy-in moving. That process also surfaced a real tension between leadership's vision for the profile and what research was telling us. Our CEO had a clear point of view on where this should go, and user research was pointing somewhere else. Closing that gap meant translating research into terms leadership cared about, not "users prefer this," but why it mattered for trust, conversion, and hire rate.

05 · Scope

Keeping what freelancers earned, changing how clients saw it

The direction ended up brighter and more colourful than the existing page, but every badge and feature freelancers had already earned carried over, so returning users wouldn't lose anything in the switch.

Based on ongoing feedback, the design centred on inline editing instead of a separate settings flow, a portfolio-style layout for past work, and visual trust cues, badges, completed work, ratings, built to do their job fast for a client seeing that freelancer for the first time.

06 · Results

A strong internal reception, without a launch to measure

I moved on from Freelancer.com before this shipped, so I don't have launch numbers to point to. What I do have is what came out of talking to users.

Freelancers wanted to edit their profile in place instead of getting routed to a separate settings page. They wanted it to read like an actual portfolio, not a directory listing. Clients new to the platform kept coming back to trust, wanting to feel confident about who they were hiring before committing to anything.

Those conversations and feedback shaped the direction of the redesign, and the core decisions from this phase became the foundation the next phase of work was built on.
