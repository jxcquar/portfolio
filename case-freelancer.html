Freelancer.com

Optimising user profiles to attract more projects and hires

FREELANCER.COM

Freelancer.com is an award-winning marketplace trusted by 71+ million users around the world.

Product Designer | Jan 2023 – Jul 2023

01 — The problem

Freelancer.com’s profiles hadn’t changed in years, and it looked it.

[ Image ] Example of a current user profile

The design dated back to the early 2000s, at a point where the platform needed to look modern enough to attract new clients and, more importantly, earn their trust before they’d commit to hiring someone off it.

Two earlier attempts to redesign the page had already stalled, not for lack of good ideas, but because it’s a hard sell to get engineering time allocated to a page that doesn’t directly move revenue.

02 — The solution

Shaping the experience inside a structure that was already set

I led phase one of the revamp, working within a direction our senior design lead had already set, and built inside Freelancer’s existing Bits design system rather than starting from a blank canvas. The page’s overall structure was already decided before I joined. What was mine to shape was the actual experience inside it: how the layout felt as a client scrolled through a freelancer’s profile, what caught their eye first, and how that sequence built trust rather than just listing information.

My product manager had already sketched a few early ideas. I turned those into low-fidelity wireframes I could actually put in front of people, mapping the page’s structure before touching any visual detail. Sharing wireframes that early meant stakeholders were shaping the direction from day one, instead of reacting to something finished later.

Once the core concept had support, I moved to mid-fidelity wireframes to pressure-test navigation and flow before committing to visual design. That round surfaced two real changes: simplifying the manage-portfolio flow, and adding a clear call-to-action on the homepage. Testing the flow before the visuals meant we weren’t redesigning anything after the fact.

With the concept validated, I used Midjourney to generate high-fidelity landing page visuals instead of waiting on production-ready assets. That let me show a vivid, near-final version of the new profile fast, and that clarity is what got wider stakeholder buy-in moving. The direction itself leaned more vivid and colourful than the existing page, but continuity mattered just as much as improvement: every badge and feature freelancers had already earned carried over, so returning users wouldn’t feel like the redesign had cost them anything.

The real tension wasn’t between me and users. It was between leadership’s vision for the profile and what research was telling us. Our CEO had a clear point of view on direction. User research was pointing somewhere else. Closing that gap meant translating research into language leadership cared about, not “users prefer this,” but why it mattered for trust, conversion and hire rate.

That translation kept getting tested against real feedback. Freelancers wanted to edit their profile in place instead of being routed to a separate settings flow. Clients needed something that read like a professional portfolio rather than a directory listing. Newer clients especially needed to feel confident about who they were hiring before committing to anything. I refined the design around inline editing, a portfolio-style layout for past work, and visual trust cues, badges, completed work, ratings, built to do their job fast for a client seeing that freelancer for the first time.

03 — The result

The foundation the next phase of work was built on

I moved on from Freelancer.com before this shipped, so I don’t have launch numbers to point to here. What I do have is the internal reception: the direction landed well with the team, and the core decisions from this phase, the portfolio-style layout, inline editing, and the trust-focused visual language, became the foundation the next phase of work was built on.
